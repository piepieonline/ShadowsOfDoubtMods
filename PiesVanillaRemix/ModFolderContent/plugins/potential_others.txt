Part 1 — Filling the commercial tower
Tenants that read as "tower floor" but aren't offices

The trick is finding things that plausibly rent a floor plate but have a completely different room grammar than desks-and-a-breakroom:

Records-heavy (gives the player a reason to break in, which offices currently lack):

Architecture / drafting firm — flat files full of blueprints. Steal or buy floorplans for other buildings in the city. Instantly the most gameplay-relevant office type possible.
Credit bureau / records service — microfiche readers, filing warrens, a terminal that answers "who is this person financially."
Employment / temp agency — a directory of who works where, useful for tracking down a name you only half have.
Insurance adjuster — claims files, photos of accidents, motive material (who benefits from a death).
Notary / small law office — wills, contracts, a document safe. Wills are murder motives you can physically find.
Alarm monitoring / security firm — holds keys and door codes for half the district. Very juicy, very lootable.

Loud/weird spatial layouts:

Radio station — soundproofed booths, tape archive, one DJ alone in the building from midnight to six.
Newspaper bureau — a clippings "morgue," a darkroom, one editor working late.
Computer bureau / data center — 80s mainframe room, raised floor, cold, humming, almost no people. Great contrast.
Telephone answering service or switchboard — rows of operators, call slips, night shift.
Photo studio / photo lab — darkroom, negatives, the classic blackmail engine.
Print shop — heavy machinery, and the obvious forgery angle.

Non-commercial floors: dentist, physiotherapist, veterinary clinic, dance studio, martial arts dojo, union hall, small chapel, tanning salon, storage lockers, talent agency, travel agency, import/export front company (a nearly empty floor with crates and one desk).

Making generic offices read differently

Even if you keep MediumOffice as one type, variation can come from four cheap axes:

1. Sector tag. Assign each office a sector — accounting, ad agency, debt collection, shipping, telemarketing, real estate — and let that drive job titles, document types on desks, wall decor, and what's in the boss's drawer. Same layout, different smell.

2. Signature room. Give every office exactly one distinguishing room: a vault, a mail room, a boardroom with a slide projector, a server closet, a sample room, a smoking lounge, a bunk room. That single deviation does more for memorability than fifty new props.

3. Security tier. A debt collection office has barred windows, a safe, and a baseball bat behind the desk. An ad agency has an unlocked door, a liquor cabinet, and a couch someone sleeps on. Security posture is characterization.

4. Occupancy schedule. A telemarketing floor that only lives at night, an accountancy that's dead except at tax time, a firm where one person is always there at 3am. Right now every office is empty on the same schedule, which is probably the biggest single cause of sameyness — it means the player only ever experiences them one way.

5. Layout archetype. Bullpen (open desks), warren (private offices off a corridor), client-facing (big lobby, small back), executive suite (three enormous rooms). Four floor grammars, procedurally mixed with the sector tag, gets you a lot of perceived variety.
